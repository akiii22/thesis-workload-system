# Problem Definition

## 1. Problem

Thesis and capstone projects are often completed by groups of students who
must divide a large amount of work among themselves. However, teams often
manage this division through group chats, verbal agreements, spreadsheets,
or simple task lists.

These methods make it difficult to clearly see how work is distributed
among members, what each member is currently responsible for, what work
has been completed, and what work is still pending.

As the project progresses, this can lead to uneven workload distribution,
missed deadlines, unclear responsibilities, and difficulty determining
each member's actual contribution to the project.

## 2. Target Users

The primary users are students working in groups on:

- Undergraduate thesis projects
- Capstone projects
- Software development projects
- Other major academic group projects

The system is primarily designed for student groups of approximately
3–6 members, with a group leader responsible for coordinating the work.

A secondary user may be a thesis or capstone adviser who needs visibility
into the team's progress and workload.

## 3. Current Workflow

A typical thesis or capstone group may currently follow a workflow similar
to the following:

1. The group discusses the project requirements.
2. The members decide which parts of the project each person will handle.
3. Tasks are usually assigned through group conversations, meetings,
   spreadsheets, documents, or messaging applications.
4. Members work independently on their assigned responsibilities.
5. Members provide updates through group chats or meetings.
6. Completed work is collected and reviewed by the group.
7. Tasks may be reassigned or changed as the project progresses.
8. The group continues this process until the project is completed.

## 4. Problems With the Current Workflow

The current workflow can create several problems:

- It is difficult to see how work is distributed across the entire team.
- Responsibilities can become unclear when tasks change or are reassigned.
- Progress updates can become inconsistent.
- Deadlines may be missed because unfinished work is not clearly visible.
- Members may have different amounts of work without the group realizing it.
- It can be difficult to keep a clear record of what each member contributed.
- Group leaders may need to repeatedly ask members for updates.
- Information about tasks and progress can become scattered across
  different applications and conversations.

  ## 5. Proposed Solution

We propose a web-based workload and contribution management system
designed specifically for thesis and capstone groups.

The system will allow teams to organize responsibilities, assign and
track tasks, monitor deadlines and progress, and maintain a record of
member contributions in one centralized system.

The goal is to make the team's workload and progress more visible so
that members and group leaders can better coordinate their work.

## 6. MVP

The initial version of the system will include:

- User registration and authentication
- Creation of a thesis/capstone project
- Adding project members
- Creating and assigning tasks
- Task status and progress tracking
- Task deadlines
- Workload visibility across team members
- Contribution/activity history
- Project and member dashboards

## 7. Out of Scope

The initial version will not include:

- Online payments
- Real-time chat
- Video conferencing
- AI-generated task assignments
- AI-based workload evaluation
- File storage
- Mobile applications
- Complex project management integrations
- Automated grading
- Student performance scoring
- Public social features

## 8. Success Criteria

The system should allow a thesis or capstone group to:

1. Clearly see who is responsible for each task.
2. See the current progress of project work.
3. Identify unfinished or overdue work.
4. Understand how work is distributed among members.
5. Review a history of member contributions.
6. Reduce the need to manually track project progress through
   scattered conversations and documents.
